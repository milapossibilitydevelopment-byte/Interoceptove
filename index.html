Ride the wave

Short exercises that bring on the body feelings anxiety can cause, like a racing heart, dizziness or a tight throat. You do each one for one minute and notice what happens. Over time your brain learns these feelings are uncomfortable, not dangerous.

How it works

Panic and anxiety are fueled by three things: scary thoughts about body sensations, a body that has learned to pair those sensations with fear, and avoiding the sensations or the places they show up. These exercises work on all three.

  1. Predict. Before you start, write what your mind says will happen and rate your anxiety from 0 to 10.
  2. Do it for one full minute. Make the sensations as strong as you can. No distractions, no water sips, no sitting down early.
  3. Notice and rate. Pick the sensations you felt, how strong they were, and how much they felt like your real anxiety or panic.
  4. Check your prediction. Write what actually happened. That gap is the new learning.
  5. Repeat. Practice the exercises that feel most like your anxiety until your fear drops by about half, then practice them in places you have been avoiding.
  • The sensations themselves are not the problem. The fear of them is.
  • Feeling uncomfortable during practice means it is working.
  • You can stop at any time. Try to stay with it anyway.
